In 1995, General Motors (NYSE: GM) pioneered connected car service when OnStar debuted. Since then, the service has expanded to include modern conveniences such as concierge services, remote unlock and a theft tracking system. Now in December 2015, the automotive giant will roll out its latest OnStar innovation -- proactive vehicle diagnostics.


Dubbed “Proactive Alerts,” the system monitors a vehicle’s battery power, fuel pump and starter, alerting the OnStar subscriber to an issue when one is about to occur. For instance, instead of heading out to your vehicle in the morning only to find out your battery is dead, Proactive Alerts will send you an alert that will tell you when your battery power is getting low and it’s time for a new battery.




The system works via a vehicle’s 4G LTE connection and an owner must have both an active OnStar and Wi-Fi connection in order to use the system. Currently there are 171,000 OnStar subscribers in Georgia. A trial subscription to OnStar’s Guidance Plan, which includes emergency, security and diagnostic services, is included with every 2016 model sold.


4G LTE is now available in many 2015 General Motors models and the company plans to roll out the connectivity option to most 2016 model year vehicles. When purchasing a vehicle, 99 percent of customers opt-in to activate the connection. And of those, 70 percent bought a recurring monthly data plan.

In an effort to alleviate concerns GM may be data mining with the new system, OnStar Marketing Manager Rich Martinek assured me last week that, “cyber security is just as important as privacy” to General Motors, confirming OnStar and GM do not “know where you’re at unless you initiate contact” via the push of the OnStar button.


Martinek sees this latest OnStar subscription benefit as a move to make true on the automaker’s goal saying, “we want people to love their cars.”
