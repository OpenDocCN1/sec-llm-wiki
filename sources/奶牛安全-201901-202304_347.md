Leaks of personal information by an organization can cause large financial losses.

The costs commonly include

*   Investigating the incident and remediating the problem
*   Contacting affected individuals to inform them about the incident
*   Penalties and fines to regulatory agencies
*   Contractual liabilities
*   Mitigating expenses
*   Direct damages to affected individuals

The most common cause of data breach for a business is a lack of awareness and discipline among employees—an overwhelming majority of all leaks are the result of negligence.

The more complex the environment and types of media used, the more communication and training that are required to ensure that the environment is well protected.

## **Data Leak Prevention**

Data leak prevention (DLP) comprises the actions that organizations take to prevent **unauthorized external parties** from **gaining access** to **sensitive** data. That definition has some key terms. First, the data has to be considered **sensitive**. Second, DLP is concerned with **external parties**. Finally, the external party gaining access to our sensitive data must be **unauthorized** to do so.

data loss means we do not know where the data is, while data leak means that the confidentiality of the data has been Compromised.

### General Approaches to DLP

One important principle is the integration of DLP with our risk management processes.

*   **Data Inventories**

A good approach is to find and characterize all the data in your organization before you even look at DLP solutions.

*   **Data Flows**

Understanding data flows at this intersection between business and IT is critical to implementing DLP.

A better approach is to use a variety of sensors tuned to specific data flows.

*   **Data Protection Strategy**

Steganography is the process of hiding data within other data such that it is difficult or impossible to detect the hidden content.

The following are some key areas to consider when developing data protection strategies:

**Backup and recovery, Data life cycle, Physical security, Security culture, Privacy, Organizational change**

*   **Implementation,  Testing, and Tuning**

The following are some aspects of a possible solution that most organizations will want to consider when comparing competing products:

**Sensitive data awareness,  Policy engine,  Interoperability,  Accuracy**

Once we select a DLP solution, the next interrelated tasks are integration, testing, and tuning.

Verifying that authorized processes are not hampered by the DLP solution is fairly straightforward if we have already inventoried our data and the authorized flows.

Testing the second critical element, that the DLP solution prevents unauthorized flows, requires a bit more work and creativity. A useful tool in documenting these types of activities is called the misuse case. Misuse cases describe threat actors and the tasks they want to perform on the system.

Finally, we must remember that everything changes.

### Network DLP

Network DLP (NDLP) applies data protection policies to data in motion.

Resiliency is the ability to deal with challenges, damage, and crises and bounce back to normal or near-normal condition in short order. It is an important element of security in general and of DLP in particular.

### Endpoint DLP

Endpoint DLP (EDLP) applies protection policies to data at rest and data in use.

### Hybrid DLP

Another approach to DLP is to deploy both NDLP and EDLP across the enterprise. Obviously, this approach is the costliest and most complex. For organizations that can afford it, however, it offers the best coverage.
