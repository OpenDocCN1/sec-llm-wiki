Privacy Amplification in Sampling Schemes 

Abstract

Sampling schemes are fundamental tools in statistics and algorithm design. A fundamental result in differential privacy is that a differentially private mechanism run on a simple random sample of a population provides higher privacy guarantees than the same algorithm run on the entire population. This property has been used to design sample-efficient algorithms. In this talk we will present a general methodology to reason about this phenomenon for different sampling scheme, including data dependent ones. We will show how different privacy amplification results can be understood through the idea of probabilistic coupling. We will also show that complex data dependent sampling schemes often do not amplify privacy, and in some cases they can actually result in privacy degradation.

  

BIO

Marco Gaboardi is an associate professor at Boston University.  Prior to joining Boston University he was an assistant professor at the University at Buffalo, SUNY, and at the University of Dundee, Scotland. Marco received his PhD from the University of Torino, Italy, and the Institute National Polytechnique de Lorraine, France. He has been a visiting scholar at the University of Pennsylvania, at Harvard University’s CRCS center, and at the Simons’ institute at UC Berkeley. He is a recipient of the NSF CAREER award and of a EU Marie Curie Fellowship. Marco's research is in programming languages, formal verification, and in differential privacy.
