The Emerging Threats Towards

Mobile Computing Platforms

Abstract

Mobile computing platforms like smartphones are increasingly used to supplement users' daily computing needs, as well as to deal with sensitive data, such as financial information, work emails, and health records. The idiosyncrasies of mobile computing platforms, coupled with the increased abilities of miscreants, have made such platforms an increasingly popular target of web-related attacks. Systematic testing of potential attack surfaces and capabilities for attackers is instrumental to identifying targeted attacks (through the Web) towards such platforms, as well as mitigating and defending against a new class of mobile-specific attacks.   

In this talk, I will describe our efforts to improve security by characterizing novel Web-related threats on mobile platforms other than traditional desktop ones. First, I will introduce an automated, browser-agnostic, dynamic-analysis testing framework (called Hindsight), which has been used to evaluate security issues of mobile browsers and malicious websites on large scale. I will illustrate Hindsight by showing a longitudinal study of User Interface security across mobile browsers. Second, I will describe our study of security-mechanism support for web-application attacks (e.g., XSS, CSRF, clickjacking) on thousands of mobile browsers by compiling a test suite consisting of nearly 400 test cases. I will also demonstrate how mobile operating systems can affect the security level of mobile browsers. Finally, I will change the vantage point and describe how attackers abuse Traffic Distribution Systems (TDS) to differentiate users and run targeted cybercrime activities. I conclude this talk by highlighting my research plan regarding using automated testing to capture novel security issues on mobile computing platforms.  

  

BIO

Meng Luo is a postdoctoral research associate in the Khoury College of Computer Sciences at the Northeastern University of the United States. She obtained her Ph.D. in Computer Science from The State University of New York at Stony Brook, under the supervision of Prof. Nick Nikiforakis. Her research focuses on ensuring the security and privacy of Web browsing on different mobile computing platforms. Her work takes the large-scale testing and measurement approach to uncover new security issues in mobile and web applications, online advertising platforms, and other online services. She earned a B.E. degree in Information Security in 2015 at Wuhan University, and she is the recipient of the Special Chair Fellowship from the Department of Computer Science at The State University of New York at Stony Brook (2015). Her research has appeared in many top-tier venues such as CCS, NDSS, WWW, and TNNLS.
