Uncovering New Threats on IoT Device Access and Management

Abstract

With the increasing popularity of the Internet of Things (IoT), many IoT cloud platforms have emerged to help the IoT manufacturers connect their devices to their users. Serving the device-user interactions are the protocols for IoT communication and access control. In this talk, I will introduce our new security analysis on the IoT protocols and systems, which is published in IEEE S&P 2020 and USENIX Security 2020. The first part of the work talks about the general messaging protocols on IoT clouds (e.g., AWS IoT) for device-user communication. Those protocols, for example, MQTT, which are typically not designed to work in the adversarial environment of IoT, introduce new risks and attacks in the IoT systems. The second part of the work analyzes access delegation mechanisms developed by IoT clouds, which we found are heterogeneous and ad-hoc in the wild, enabling practical new attacks. Those problems were found systematically using a formal verification based approach, and caused due to the lack of a standardized, proper delegation protocol suited for IoT systems and scenarios. In this talk, I plan to introduce the new security problems we found, and discuss their causes, corresponding new security challenges, and possible solutions.

  

BIO

 Luyi Xing (邢璐祎)， Assistant Professor of Indiana University Bloomington. He published 17 papers in top 4 security conferences and 3 at Black Hat. He served on the Program Committee of ACM CCS and NDSS. His current research focus is security analysis on IoT and mobile systems (both iOS and Android), which has led to the discovery of many design/logic vulnerabilities on commercial and open-source systems. His research broadly involves protocol design and analysis, program analysis, formal verification, machine learning/NLP, etc. He is among the very first few practitioners in iOS security research. What his group discovered are typically fundamental design weaknesses in real systems, versus implementation bugs/mistakes. With in-depth understanding of systems and innovative, in-depth cause/challenge analysis, his group generalizes new design principles and develops solutions to protect real systems of Apple, Google, Amazon/AWS, Microsoft, Samsung, IBM, Alibaba, PayPal, Firefox, Tencent and much more. His research has been reported by Time, CNN, Forbes, Mirror, Fox News, Yahoo, CNET, The Register, Sina, 163, Sohu and more.
