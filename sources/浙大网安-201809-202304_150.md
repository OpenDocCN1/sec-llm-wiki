An Elementary Introduction to Wireless Sensing and Security Applications

Abstract

Wireless signals are everywhere in our life, including Wi-Fi, 4G/LTE and space radio (e.g., X/Gamma-Rays). Wireless signals are traditionally designated for communication, and later on, exploited as sensing tools to understand the physical world. Recently, how to leverage or retrofit existing communication devices for novel sensing applications becomes a drastically growing topic. Since 2010, more than 10,000 papers of wireless sensing were published in ACM and IEEE communities. Last year (2019), Google formally introduced the feature of Motion Sense, a radio signal sensing technology, in its flagship smartphone Pixel 4.

In this talk, we will first go through the recent history, fundamental backgrounds and novel applications of wireless sensing. And then, we will demonstrate and discuss a few frontier research works in wireless sensing from the security perspective. In the end, I will share my thoughts about future research on Wireless Sensing Security.

  

BIO

Dr. Xu is an Associate Professor of Computer Science and Engineering Department in the State University of New York (SUNY) at Buffalo, where he founds and directs the ESC (Embedded Sensing and Computing) Group. He has published over 160 technical papers, co-authored 2 books and is a named inventor on many international and U.S. patents. He received the Ph.D. degree from University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) in 2013. He received both M.S. degree in 2008 and B.S. degree in 2006 from Zhejiang University (both with honor), China.
