HISA: Hardware Isolation-based Secure Architecture for CPU-FPGA Embedded Systems

BIO

Mengmei Ye received her BS degree in the CSE Department at University of Nebraska-Lincoln in 2016. Currently, she is a 4th year PhD student in the ECE Department at Rutgers University. She primarily focuses on addressing security challenges in CPU-FPGA heterogeneous systems. Her work has been recognized with a Best Paper Award at IEEE ICCD 2016, a Best Paper Nomination at IEEE HOST 2018, and a “Samsung Breakthroughs That Matter” award at MIT Hacking Medicine Grand Hack 2019.

Abstract

Heterogeneous CPU-FPGA systems have been shown to achieve significant performance gains in domain-specific computing. The interplay between CPU and FPGA in such a heterogeneous system may introduce cross-layer attack surfaces that compromise the data/program confidentiality and integrity. We propose a hardware isolation-based secure architecture, namely HISA, to mitigate the identified threats. HISA extends the CPU-based hardware isolation primitive to the heterogeneous FPGA components and achieves security guarantees in the isolated secure environment. However, there exist two primary challenges that we must address to adopt HISA into practice effectively. The one is the programming challenge about how to partition the regular software programs into HISA. The other is the security challenge about how to verify the integrity of FPGA IP cores at runtime. In this talk, I will introduce our current solutions (i.e., a programming solution and a security solution) to address the aforementioned challenges. Specifically, the programming solution is an automatic program slicer, namely TZSlicer, to partition the original program into HISA. The security solution is a runtime hardware security verifier by leveraging approximate computing, namely ApproVer.
