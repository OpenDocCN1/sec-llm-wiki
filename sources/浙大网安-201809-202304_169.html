Bottom Up Security on Execution Stacks: Top Down Verification of a Separation Micro-Kernel for Multi-Core Architectures

BIO

David Sanan is a Senior Research Fellow at the Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore. He received his Ph.D. in Software Engineering and Artificial Intelligence from the University of Malaga, Spain. In 2015, he joined NTU, where he is leading a group researching on formal methods applied to security.

He is currently working on the formalization and verification of a separation micro-kernel aiming at multi-core architectures. His research interest includes formal methods, software verification, programming languages, and embedded systems. 

Abstract

The increasing presence of IoT devices and Autonomous Systems (from drones to cars) in our lives has brought significant concern regarding the security and privacy of such systems. The Securify project aims to provide a secure execution stack for embedded systems from the hardware to applications, including the system kernel.

While general operating systems like Linux and Windows fail to provide a secure environment to the applications running on them, other concepts such as the separation micro-kernel conceived by John Rushby, can provide enhanced security. The relative small size of the implementation of these micro-kernels make feasible to apply formal verification to ensure their correctness w.r.t. functionality and a number of desired security properties. Whilst formal verification has already been conducted on micro-kernels running on single core CPU architecture, verification of multi-core CPU architectures present new challenges.

In this talk he will illustrate the methodologies, techniques, and tools used for the verification of XtratuM a state-of-the-art micro-kernel.
