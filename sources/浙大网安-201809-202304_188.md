GUARDER: 

A Tunable Secure Allocator

**BIO**

Dr. Tongping Liu is an assistant professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at  the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where he also got his Ph.D. degree but from the Department of Computer Science. After the graduation, he worked at the University of Texas at San Antonio for five years. His research spans Runtime System, Operating System, Programming Language, Compiler, and Distributed System, with the focus of practically improving the security, reliability, and performance of software systems. He received the Google Faculty Award in 2015. 

**Abstract**

Heap vulnerability is still an on-going threat to the software, and randomization is believed to be an important method to defend heap vulnerabilities. However, existing heap allocators either have low/zero randomization entropy, or cannot provide stable security guarantees, where their entropies vary by object size classes, execution phases, inputs, or applications. This talk will talk about a novel secure allocator — GUARDER. GUARDER provides an unprecedented level of security guarantee,  but only imposing less than 3% overhead.  GUARDER also provides an additional feature that allows users to customize security based on their performance budget, without changing code or even recompiling. The combination of high security, low overhead, and flexibility makes GUARDER a practical solution for different deployed environment.
