R&D on Privacy-Preserving Data Mining

Abstract

Recently, computational outsourcing using cloud services is getting popular for data analysis, and many cloud sourcing providers provide machine learning platforms. On the other hand, there still remains a big hurdle to analyze personal data on cloud services because the leakage of personal information is a critical issue. In order to address the issue, the homomorphic encryption (HE) which allows computation on encrypted data is considered as a useful technique because it can delegate the complicated calculation operation to the semi-reliable cloud server without information leakage. Especially, the inner product of vectors is one of the most basic and useful operations in statistical calculations and machine learning. In this talk, we first introduce our recent study on homomorphic encryption technique of constructing generic yet efficient secure inner product, then present our recent works on privacy-preserving machine learning (e.g., privacy-preserving logistic regression, three-layer feedforward neural networks, and so on).

  Bio 

Lihua Wang received the M.S degree in mathematics from Harbin Institute of Technology, P.R. China, in 1994, and the Ph.D degree in engineering from University of Tsukuba, Japan, in 2006, respectively. After completing the doctoral course, she worked as a researcher at University of Tsukuba, and then a senior researcher at National Institute of Information and Communications Technology. She is currently Associate Professor at Graduate School of engineering, Kobe University, Japan. Her current research interests are cryptography and privacy-preserving data mining.
